Know your furikake

What is Nori Komi Furikake?

Nori komi furikake is the classic, simplest style of furikake, a dry Japanese rice seasoning made with crisp nori seaweed and toasted sesame. Furikake means "to sprinkle over," and that's exactly how you use it: a shake over hot rice, poke, eggs or popcorn.

Unlike bonito (katsuo) or egg (noritama) blends, nori komi skips the fish and egg, so it stays mild, nutty and easy to put on everything. In Hawai'i it's a pantry staple, and our island-style blend is the one Braddah Tim grew up sprinkling on everything.

Flavor profile

What does it taste like?

Slightly sweet, gently salty and full of toasty, sea-breeze umami, with a light crunch. No heat and no fishiness, just 'ono.

Customers describe it as a "very fresh ocean flavor perfectly balanced with sweet, sesame and slightly smoky flavors."

Nori Komi Furikake FAQs

Is Pono Nori Komi Furikake gluten-free?

Yes. It's labeled gluten-free and contains no wheat, soy sauce or barley ingredients. If you have celiac disease, always check the jar in hand, since recipes can change.

Is it vegan?

It's made with plant-based ingredients and contains no fish flakes, bonito or egg.

Does it contain MSG?

[Answer from the current label.] If the label lists MSG: "Yes, our Nori Komi includes a small amount of monosodium glutamate for that savory umami kick." If it lists yeast extract and no MSG: "There's no added MSG on our label. The umami comes from seaweed, sesame and yeast extract."

How should I store it, and how long does it last?

Keep the lid tight and store it in a cool, dry pantry away from sunlight and steam. Use it by the best-by date on the jar. For the crispiest crunch, finish an opened jar within a few months.

What do you eat nori komi furikake with?

Rice first and always, then musubi, poke, sushi, eggs, popcorn, salmon, chicken, roasted veggies, noodles and furikake Chex mix.

What's the difference between nori komi and nori goma?

Both are nori-and-sesame furikake. "Goma" means sesame, so nori goma blends tend to be more sesame-forward, while nori komi is the classic nori-forward blend. Recipes vary by brand. Our Nori Goma is coming soon.

What's the difference between Hawaiian and Japanese furikake?

Furikake is Japanese. In Hawai'i it became a local staple used island-style: on poke, musubi, plate-lunch rice, furikake chicken and furikake Chex mix. Some Hawaiian brands add local ingredients like Hawaiian sea salt. Ours is a nori komi blend from Braddah Tim's island recipes.
